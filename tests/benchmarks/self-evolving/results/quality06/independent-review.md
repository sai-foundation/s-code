## Exposure limits the interpretation

Training produced **flow 0, queue 1, report 0** saved lessons. Only the three queue learned attempts received verified distilled context: 54 outbound requests, zero invalid payloads. All six flow/report off-versus-learned initial request pairs were byte-identical, and neither family received later distilled context. Differences between those empty-treatment arms cannot establish memory benefit.

The only exposed family, queue, passed 3/3 in every arm. Its learned H12 tokens per verified success were 250,423.50 versus off 174,440.42—**43.56% higher**. Consequently, the favorable aggregate result does not demonstrate that memory improved efficiency. These diagnostics do not change the original screen, exclusions or weights; all empty outcomes remain.

## Workflow and accounting scope

This experiment trained **once per family**, froze the resulting source/lessons/profile/raw corpus, and ran every learned development attempt in **reuse mode** with a fresh isolated profile. H1/H4/H12/H24 amortize that initial training and reflection. They do **not** measure continuing learn-mode reflection after every subsequent task, cumulative learning over 12 tasks or all costs of ongoing learning.

Common training used 417,935 tokens; reflection used 20,393, for 438,328 training tokens and $0.21811832. Common training is allocated equally to all arms and reflection only to learned. Actual experiment totals, including all training and failed attempts, were **6,381,468 tokens and $2.99770392**. Agent duration totaled 3,204.43 seconds; summed provider request duration was 2,819.41 seconds. Lifecycle-equivalent costs are separate amortization scenarios, not literal spending.
